Cloud Platform · Reliability · Product Decision

When “not yet” is the product decision.

A cloud platform service was already live, but not yet reliable enough to become the dependency for another customer-facing feature. The challenge was to balance customer commitments, production stability and long-term platform health.

02 · My decision

Do not build the feature on an unstable dependency — yet.

The customer value of the new feature remained valid. The decision was about sequence: first create enough evidence and reliability to make extending the platform a responsible choice.

Decision
No — not now.

I would not commit the new customer-facing feature onto the service in its current state. The immediate priority is to investigate and stabilise the platform capability, then reopen the feature decision with a more reliable technical and planning basis.

The decision is not “stability instead of customer value”. It is “stability before adding another dependency and a larger blast radius”.
Why this decision?
  • There was already a real customer signal of instability.
  • The team could not yet isolate failures reliably.
  • Monitoring and alerting were incomplete.
  • Error behaviour was not sufficiently tested.
  • Adding a feature would increase complexity before the baseline was understood.
Decision criteria

I would make the trade-off explicit.

The platform decision needs to balance customer value with reliability, operational risk and the cost of making the problem harder to diagnose later.

Customer valueIs the feature still strategically valuable?
ReliabilityCan the platform support another dependency safely?
ObservabilityCan failures be detected and isolated?
Blast radiusWhat becomes harder if we extend now?

Capacity

Use the available capacity to reduce uncertainty first.

30% free
70% already planned

The 30% free capacity is not automatically “feature capacity”. It is a decision budget. I would use it first to understand the failure modes, close the biggest observability gaps and create a realistic stabilisation estimate.

03 · Three milestones

Investigate → Stabilise → Extend.

The decision becomes easier to communicate when the work is framed as three explicit milestones with a clear gate between them.

01~1 sprint

Investigate

Build enough understanding to know what we are stabilising and how large the problem really is.

Review the recent incident and known failure patterns Identify monitoring and alerting gaps Map downstream dependencies Assess missing failure-mode tests Estimate stabilisation scope with the Technical Lead
Gate: Can we explain the current risks and define what “stable enough” means?
02~2 sprints

Stabilise

Close the critical reliability gaps before introducing another customer dependency.

Fix critical defects and root causes Complete relevant monitoring and alerting Test important failure scenarios Improve operational visibility and recovery paths Define a readiness check before extension
Gate: Is the service observable, testable and reliable enough to extend?
03~2 sprints + release buffer

Extend

Reopen the feature decision once the dependency has a stronger operational baseline.

Reconfirm customer scope and value Plan the feature on a more credible baseline Implement with explicit dependency tests Validate before production release Monitor post-release behaviour
Outcome: Feature delivery with a lower platform and customer risk.
Timeframe note. Sprint durations shown here are the planning assumptions I used in the interview discussion, not facts provided by the case. The point was to create a transparent sequence and a credible re-planning moment, not to claim certainty before investigation.
Communication

One decision, three different conversations.

Project Manager A

“Not now — and here is the next decision point.”

The feature remains valuable, but I would not commit it onto the current dependency. We first stabilise the service, then re-plan the feature from a more reliable baseline.
Project Manager B

“Your incident changes the priority.”

The recent instability is evidence that the platform needs focused reliability work. We prioritise investigation and stabilisation and communicate progress against explicit readiness criteria.
Technical Lead / Architect

“Turn technical concern into testable readiness.”

I take the technical warning seriously, but translate it together with Engineering into observable risks, concrete work and a shared definition of when the service is safe to extend.

05 · Reflection

The reasoning was stronger than the way I presented it.

The interview feedback was not that the underlying decision logic was wrong. It was that I should have made the current state, the “not now” decision and the sequence of actions much easier to follow.

What I did well

I looked beyond the feature request.

I connected customer impact, platform reliability, technical uncertainty and long-term operating conditions instead of treating the requests as two competing backlog items.

Used the real customer incident as evidenceProtected the platform from a larger blast radiusKept the feature valuable, but changed the sequenceProposed structural improvements, not only a one-off fix
What I changed afterwards

Make the decision visible earlier.

I had explored the relevant risks, but the structure could have been sharper. Today I would state the current state and the decision first, then show the milestones and reasoning behind it.

Start with the known current stateSeparate facts, assumptions and open questionsSay “no — not yet” explicitlyShow a small number of clear milestonesEnd with the next decision point and success criteria

The missing piece in my original presentation was not the reasoning itself. It was the reading direction: first understand the current state, then evaluate options, then make the decision and the next step explicit.

Good platform decisions are often sequencing decisions.

Customer value, delivery pressure and technical health do not have to be treated as opposing camps. The PO’s job is to make the dependency visible, choose the right order and create a credible path back to value.
